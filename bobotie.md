# Bobotie

*South African curried beef bake with a soft egg-custard topping, studded with bay leaves. Sweet-savory, gently spiced, and traditionally served over yellow rice with chutney.*

## Ingredients

- 2 lb ground beef (or half beef, half lamb)
- 2 slices white or sourdough bread
- ½ cup milk, for soaking the bread
- 1 large yellow onion, finely chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 tbsp butter
- 2 tbsp curry powder (mild or medium)
- 1 tsp ground turmeric
- 1 tsp ground ginger
- 1½ tsp salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 2 tbsp apricot jam or fruit chutney
- 2 tbsp white wine vinegar or lemon juice
- 1 tsp brown sugar
- ½ cup raisins or sultanas
- ⅓ cup slivered almonds
- 3 large eggs
- 1 cup milk, for the topping
- 6 bay leaves (or fresh lemon leaves)

## Steps

1. **Soak the bread.** Tear the bread into a small bowl and pour over the milk. Let it soak while you cook everything else — you'll squeeze it out later and save the milk. *(timer: 10 min)*
2. **Soften the aromatics.** Melt butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and cook until soft and translucent. Stir in the garlic, the curry powder, turmeric, and ginger and let the spices bloom for about a minute until fragrant. *(timer: 8 min)*
3. **Brown the meat.** Add the ground beef, breaking it up with a spoon, and cook until it's no longer pink and any liquid has mostly cooked off. *(timer: 10 min)*
4. **Build the filling.** Squeeze the soaked the bread (reserve the milk) and crumble it into the pan. Stir in the jam or chutney, the vinegar or lemon juice, brown sugar, the raisins, the almonds, salt, and black pepper. Simmer a few minutes so the flavors meld, then taste and adjust. Preheat the oven to 350°F (180°C). *(timer: 5 min)*
5. **Pack the dish.** Spoon the meat into a greased baking dish (about 9×9 or 8×11) and press it into an even, compact layer.
6. **Make the custard top.** Whisk the eggs with the topping milk plus the reserved soaking milk, a pinch of salt, and a pinch of turmeric for color. Pour evenly over the meat, then tuck the bay leaves upright into the surface.
7. **Bake.** Bake at 350°F (180°C) until the custard is just set and golden on top — it should no longer jiggle in the center. *(timer: 35 min)*
8. **Rest and serve.** Let it rest before cutting so the custard firms up. Discard the bay leaves and serve over yellow (turmeric) rice with extra chutney on the side. *(timer: 10 min)*

## Notes

From your market list, the ground beef is Bowdish (a 2 lb pack is exactly right), eggs from 7 Mile Farm, and garlic from Sauvie Island Growers. The rest — milk, bread, curry powder, turmeric, ginger, vinegar, raisins, and almonds — are pantry items. The market doesn't carry apricot jam, but the traditional move is apricot; their raspberry jam or a spoon of chutney works in a pinch. Half beef / half lamb (M&P ground lamb) is a very common and excellent variation if you want to use both.

Don't skip blooming the curry powder in the fat — that's where most of the depth comes from. The custard should go on just-set and a little wobbly; overbaking makes it rubbery and weepy. Yellow rice with raisins is the canonical side, but a simple tomato-and-onion sambal or a sliced cucumber salad cuts the richness nicely.
