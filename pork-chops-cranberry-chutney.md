# Reverse-Seared Pork Chops with Cranberry-Apple Chutney

*Stamboom's herb chops cooked low in the oven, then seared hard for a minute a side, with a sweet-tart cranberry chutney and roasted Brussels sprouts. About 1 hour 15 minutes; the chutney can be made days ahead.*

## Ingredients

**Chutney** (makes about 2 cups)

- 8 oz fresh cranberries (one ½ lb bag), rinsed and picked over
- 1 Jonagold apple, peeled, cored, diced ½ inch
- 1 shallot, minced
- ½ cup packed brown sugar
- ⅓ cup apple cider vinegar
- ¼ cup water
- Zest and juice of ½ orange
- ½ tsp fennel seed, lightly crushed
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- 2 fresh thyme sprigs

**Chops and sprouts**

- 4 Stamboom Italian-herb pork chops (bone-in, about 1 inch thick; two packs)
- 1 tsp kosher salt, divided
- 1 lb Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved (quartered if large)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp neutral oil, for searing
- 2 tbsp butter
- 2 garlic cloves, smashed

## Steps

1. **Start the chutney.** Put everything for the chutney in a small saucepan and bring to a simmer over medium heat. Cook, stirring now and then, until most of the cranberries have burst and the mixture mounds on a spoon. It thickens more as it cools. Pull the thyme stems, taste, and add a pinch more salt if it reads only sweet. *(timer: 15 min)*
2. **Roast the sprouts.** Heat the oven to 425°F. Toss the Brussels sprouts with the olive oil, ½ tsp of the kosher salt and the black pepper and spread cut-side down on a sheet pan. Roast until the cut sides are deeply browned and the leaves on top are crisp. Set the pan aside; the sprouts go back in to warm at the end. *(timer: 25 min)*
3. **Season and temper the chops.** While the sprouts roast, pat the chops very dry. They come herb-seasoned, so only a light pinch of the remaining salt on each side. Set them on a wire rack over a second sheet pan and leave them at room temperature. A dry surface is what sears; a cold center is what overcooks the outside before the middle is done.
4. **Oven low.** When the sprouts come out, drop the oven to 250°F and prop the door open a minute to let it fall. Slide in the rack of chops and cook until an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part, away from the bone, reads 110°F. One-inch chops take 25–35 minutes; start checking at 20. *(timer: 25 min)* Slow heat brings the whole chop up evenly, so there's no gray band and almost no window to miss. It also dries the surface further, which is why the sear is fast.
5. **Warm the sprouts.** When the chops read about 100°F, put the sheet pan of sprouts back in the oven with them.
6. **Sear.** Heat the neutral oil in a cast-iron skillet over high heat until it just smokes. Lay in the chops and sear, pressing lightly, until deeply browned, about 1 minute. Flip, add the butter and smashed garlic, tilt the pan and baste for another minute. Hold each chop on its fat edge for 20 seconds to crisp it. Pull them at 135–138°F. *(timer: 3 min)*
7. **Rest.** Move the chops to a board and rest; they coast to 140–145°F. *(timer: 5 min)*
8. **Serve.** A chop each, a spoonful of chutney on top or beside, Brussels sprouts alongside. Pass the rest of the chutney.

## Notes

The thermometer is the whole recipe. The October version of these chops came out dry because a full sear from cold has to run long enough to cook the center, and by then the outside is well past done. Reverse searing moves almost all the cooking to the gentle oven and makes the sear about color only; with 1-inch chops the window is narrow, so check early. Thicker chops (1½ inches and up) are more forgiving if Stamboom ever has them.

The chutney keeps two weeks in the fridge and is good cold, so make it a day or two ahead if the evening is tight. It also stands in for cranberry sauce at Thanksgiving. One ½ lb bag of cranberries is exactly a batch; the fennel seed echoes the chops' seasoning and can be swapped for a pinch of cinnamon.
