# Cold Sesame Noodles

*Chilled noodles in a creamy, tangy sesame dressing with ribboned cucumber, spring onion, and shiso. No real cooking beyond boiling the noodles — a perfect hot-weather dinner.*

## Ingredients

- 12 oz dried noodles (soba, ramen, or lo mein)
- 3 tbsp tahini or natural peanut butter
- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil
- 1 tbsp honey or sugar
- 2 garlic cloves, finely grated
- 1 tsp ginger, finely grated
- 1 tbsp chili crisp or sriracha (optional)
- 3 tbsp warm water
- 1 Persian or slicing cucumber, ribboned or julienned
- 3 spring onions, thinly sliced
- 6 shiso leaves, thinly sliced (or basil)
- 2 tbsp toasted sesame seeds

## Steps

1. **Cook and chill the noodles.** Bring a pot of water to a boil and cook the noodles according to package directions until just tender. Drain and rinse thoroughly under cold running water until completely cool — this stops the cooking and rinses off surface starch so they don't clump. *(timer: 6 min)*
2. **Make the sesame dressing.** While the noodles cook, whisk together tahini, soy sauce, rice vinegar, toasted sesame oil, honey or sugar, garlic, ginger, and chili crisp or sriracha. Add warm water a little at a time until the dressing is smooth and pourable — like heavy cream. Taste and adjust: more vinegar for tang, more honey for balance, more soy for salt.
3. **Dress the noodles.** Toss the cooled, well-drained noodles with the dressing until evenly coated.
4. **Add the fresh stuff and serve.** Pile in the cucumber and most of the spring onions. Top with the shiso, the remaining spring onion, and toasted sesame seeds. Serve cold or at room temperature.

## Notes

From the market: Persian cucumber, spring onions, and shiso (or sub the mixed basil) are all in right now. To make it a full dinner, add shredded poached Tail Feather chicken or top each bowl with a jammy 7-minute egg. The dressing keeps several days in the fridge and thickens as it sits — just loosen with a splash of warm water. Scale the chili to taste; start low if you're unsure.

The cold rinse on the noodles isn't optional — warm noodles seize the dressing and clump. Make the dressing a touch looser than you think you want, because the noodles drink it up as everything sits.
