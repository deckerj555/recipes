# Tomato and Egg over Rice

*The Chinese weeknight staple — soft eggs folded into a silky tomato sauce, over rice. Fifteen minutes, one pan.*

## Ingredients

- 6 large eggs
- 1½ lb ripe tomatoes, cut into 1-inch wedges (or one 14-oz can whole peeled, drained and torn)
- 4 scallions, whites and greens separated, both sliced thin
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, finely grated (optional)
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1½ tsp sugar
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
- 3 tbsp neutral oil, divided
- ¾ tsp kosher salt, divided
- 1 tsp cornstarch mixed with 2 tbsp cold water (optional, if the sauce is thin)
- 3 cups cooked short-grain or jasmine rice

## Steps

1. **Get the rice going.** Start the rice so it's ready when the pan work is done. *(timer: 20 min)*
2. **Prep everything.** Cut the tomatoes, slice the scallions keeping whites and greens separate, beat the eggs with ¼ tsp of the salt, and mix the soy sauce, sugar and sesame oil in a small bowl. Everything happens fast from here, so have it all within reach.
3. **Soft-scramble the eggs.** Heat 2 tbsp of the neutral oil in a large skillet over medium-high until shimmering. Pour in the eggs, let them set for 10 seconds, then push them around in large folds until just barely set and still glossy. Slide them onto a plate — they finish cooking later in the sauce, and eggs scrambled in with the tomatoes from the start turn rubbery and gray. *(timer: 1 min)*
4. **Aromatics.** Add the remaining oil to the pan with the scallion whites, garlic and ginger. Stir until fragrant but not browned. *(timer: 30 sec)*
5. **Cook down the tomatoes.** Add the tomatoes and the remaining ½ tsp salt. Cook over medium-high, pressing with the spatula to help them break down, until they collapse into a loose sauce and the liquid has reduced by about a third. Stir in the soy-sugar-sesame mixture. *(timer: 6 min)*
6. **Thicken if needed.** If the sauce looks watery, stir in the cornstarch slurry and simmer until it coats a spoon. Taste for salt and sugar: it should read savory and round, not sharp. *(timer: 30 sec)*
7. **Fold the eggs back in.** Return the eggs and any juices to the pan and fold gently, just until warmed and coated. Don't stir hard — you want distinct curds, not a uniform scramble. *(timer: 30 sec)*
8. **Serve.** Spoon over the rice and scatter the scallion greens on top.

## Notes

The sugar is not optional. A teaspoon or so doesn't make the dish sweet — it rounds off the tomatoes' acidity, which is what keeps this from tasting like scrambled eggs in pasta sauce. Taste at the end and add a pinch more if it's sharp.

Canned tomatoes are fine here, and often better than pale fresh ones in late fall. Drain and tear them by hand. For fresh, a cross-hatch and 30 seconds in boiling water slips the skins off, or grate them on a box grater and discard the skin.

Kenji covers this dish in *The Wok*: [Chinese Scrambled Eggs With Tomato](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gb37bRsBxZA). Worth watching once — it moves fast enough that seeing it beats reading it.

Sourcing: scallions from Gargoyle Gardens, eggs from 7 Mile, Allegre or 3 Corners, heirloom tomatoes from Feather Oak if you want fresh.

## Variations

A handful of spinach wilted in at the end, or served over noodles instead of rice. Some versions add a splash of ketchup for body and sweetness — it sounds wrong and tastes right.
