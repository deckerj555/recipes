# Mild Japanese Pork & Kabocha Curry

*Pork shoulder simmered tender with carrots and kabocha, thickened with a mild store-bought curry block and served over rice. Built on Kenji López-Alt's one-pot method, with grated apple, ketchup and Worcestershire borrowed from his Serious Eats version. About 1 hour 20 minutes, mostly simmering.*

<!-- Based on Kenji's cooking-show curry (YouTube QhsrBz9Cceg) and his Serious Eats Japanese curry rice from scratch. -->

## Ingredients

- 900 g boneless pork shoulder (2 lb), cut into 4 cm (1½-inch) chunks
- Kosher salt
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 28 g butter (2 tbsp)
- 1 large yellow onion (about 300 g), sliced thin
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 2.5 cm (1-inch) knob ginger, minced
- 700 g low-sodium broth or water (3 cups)
- 2 carrots (about 200 g), cut into 2.5 cm (1-inch) chunks
- ½ kabocha squash (about 450 g), seeded and cut into 4 cm (1½-inch) chunks, skin on
- 1 Honeycrisp apple (about 150 g), peeled and grated
- 1 tbsp ketchup
- 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 110 g mild Japanese curry block (half a 220 g box, one tray), such as S&B Golden Curry Mild or House Vermont Curry Mild
- 140 g frozen peas (1 cup)
- Honey, to taste
- Steamed short-grain rice, sliced scallions and Japanese pickles, to serve

## Steps

1. **Start the rice and prep.** Salt the pork lightly. Cut the vegetables. *(timer: rice, per your cooker)*
2. **Brown the pork.** Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Brown the pork well on a couple of sides in two batches so it sears instead of steaming. Set it aside. *(timer: 8 min)*
3. **Onion.** Add the butter and onion and cook, scraping up the browned bits, until golden. *(timer: 8 min)*
4. **Aromatics.** Add the garlic and ginger and stir until fragrant. *(timer: 30 sec)*
5. **Simmer the pork.** Add the broth and return the pork with its juices. Cover and keep at a bare simmer until the pork is nearly tender. Shoulder needs this time; a lean cut would toughen. *(timer: 45 min)*
6. **Carrots, then kabocha.** Add the carrots, apple, ketchup and Worcestershire and simmer. Add the kabocha, cover, and simmer until it's just tender. Kabocha goes in later because it falls apart faster than carrots. *(timer: 5 min, then 10–12 min)*
7. **Melt in the block.** Take the pot off the heat. Break the curry block into pieces and stir until completely dissolved. Off the heat it melts smoothly instead of clumping. *(timer: 2 min)*
8. **Thicken.** Return to low heat and stir often while the sauce thickens; the roux can catch on the bottom. *(timer: 5 min)*
9. **Finish.** Stir in the peas and heat through. Taste, and add a little honey only if it needs it. *(timer: 2 min)*

## Notes

Curry blocks are salty, so salt only the pork and taste at the end. Brands call for different amounts of water per block; if your box asks for more than 700 g (3 cups) per tray, use the box's amount. Too thin: simmer uncovered a few minutes. Too thick: add a splash of water.

Stick with blocks labeled "mild." Vermont Curry is already sweetened with apple and honey, so it may not need honey.

Changes from Kenji: kabocha instead of potato (it thickens and sweetens the sauce); pork shoulder simmered 45 minutes instead of chicken breast for 10; a curry block instead of curry powder and flour; no raisins; no cayenne.

Pork shoulder isn't usually at the co-op; the kabocha, carrots, onion and apple are. Better the next day; thin with water when reheating.

## Beef version

Use 725–900 g (1.6–2 lb) top sirloin sliced 5 mm thick across the grain instead of pork. Sear it hard in two batches, about 1 minute per side, and set it aside. Skip the 45-minute simmer: add the carrots and apple right after the broth. Return the beef with its juices along with the peas at the end, so it stays tender. About 40 minutes total.
