# Seared Rockfish over Corn & Okra Maque Choux

*Louisiana skillet corn with okra, sweet peppers, and cream, topped with a mildly seasoned seared fillet — no heat*

## Ingredients

- 6 ears fresh corn, kernels cut off, cobs scraped
- ½ lb okra, sliced into 1/2-inch coins
- 4 slices bacon, chopped (or 3 Tbsp butter)
- 1 sweet onion, diced
- 2 sweet red peppers (Carmen or Jimmy Nardello), diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 ripe tomatoes, chopped (or 1 cup halved cherry tomatoes)
- ⅓ cup heavy cream
- 3 fresh thyme sprigs
- ¼ tsp sweet paprika or Habanada powder (optional)
- 2 spring onions, thinly sliced, to finish
- 4 rockfish fillets (6–8 oz each), patted very dry
- 1 tsp sweet paprika (for the rub)
- ½ tsp each: garlic powder, onion powder, dried thyme, dried oregano
- 1 tsp kosher salt, divided, plus more to taste
- 2 tbsp butter, plus 1 Tbsp neutral oil, for searing
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges

## Steps

1. **Prep the corn.** Cut the kernels from the corn into a wide bowl, then scrape each cob hard with the back of the knife to collect the milky pulp — that starchy "corn milk" gives maque choux its creamy body. Keep pulp and kernels together.
2. **Render the bacon.** In a large 12-inch cast-iron or heavy skillet over medium heat, cook bacon until crisp, about 5 minutes. Lift out the bacon bits and set aside, leaving the fat in the pan. *(timer: 5 min)*
3. **Brown the okra.** Raise heat to medium-high. Add okra in a single layer and cook, stirring only occasionally, until browned at the edges and dry-looking, 5–6 minutes. High, dry heat is the anti-slime step — don't rush it and don't crowd the pan. *(timer: 5 min 30 sec)*
4. **Build the base.** Add sweet onion and sweet red peppers; cook until softened, about 5 minutes. Stir in garlic cloves and cook 30 seconds until fragrant. *(timer: 5 min 30 sec)*
5. **Add corn and tomatoes.** Add the corn kernels with their scraped pulp, ripe tomatoes, fresh thyme sprigs, paprika or Habanada powder, half of kosher salt, and several grinds of black pepper. Reduce heat to medium and simmer, stirring now and then, until the corn is tender and the mixture is thick and juicy, 10–15 minutes. *(timer: 13 min)*
6. **Finish the maque choux.** Stir in heavy cream and simmer 2–3 minutes until silky. Pull the thyme stems, taste for salt, and stir the bacon back in. Cover and keep warm off the heat. *(timer: 2 min 30 sec)*
7. **Season the fish.** Mix sweet paprika and the garlic powder, onion powder, dried thyme and dried oregano with the remaining kosher salt in a small bowl. Pat rockfish fillets very dry again and coat both sides evenly with the rub.
8. **Sear the fish.** Heat butter and oil in a separate skillet over medium-high until the foam subsides. Lay in the fillets (batches if needed) and cook undisturbed 3–4 minutes until deeply golden. Flip and cook 2–3 minutes more, until the fish flakes at the thickest point (135–140°F), basting with the pan butter as it finishes. *(timer: 7 min)*
9. **Serve.** Spoon a generous bed of maque choux onto each plate, set a fillet on top, scatter with spring onions, and serve with wedges of lemon.

## Notes

Fish swaps: lingcod (denser, sears beautifully) or halibut (the classic Gulf-style stand-in) both work; skip petrale sole — too delicate for the chunky base. The Louisiana shrimp version is also great: stir shrimp into the corn for the last 3–4 minutes. Traditional recipes add cayenne; this version leaves it out entirely. Market shopping: corn, okra, sweet onions, Carmen/Jimmy Nardello peppers, garlic, tomatoes, thyme, spring onions, rockfish (Tre-Fin), and Stamboom bacon are all on the current list.

## Without okra

Drop step 3 (browning the okra); everything else stands, with three adjustments:

**Thickening.** The okra was doing real work; without it the dish comes out looser. Scrape the cobs hard — really lean on the back of the knife, that milky pulp is the body — and simmer the corn stage 5 minutes longer uncovered before the cream goes in. If it's still thin, mash a spoonful of the kernels against the side of the pan.

**Peppers.** If sweet peppers aren't available, use **two poblanos** instead (mild, and a common choice in Southwest-leaning versions), or one poblano plus a red bell for color and sweetness. Char and peel the poblanos first, same as for the chile verde.

**Browning.** Since the okra step provided most of the caramelization, let the corn sit undisturbed for two minutes when it first hits the pan before you start stirring.
