# Seattle-Style Teriyaki Sauce

*Kenji López-Alt's teriyaki-shop sauce: sake, mirin and soy simmered with aromatics, reduced and thickened to a glaze — about 2½ cups, 10 minutes active, an hour plus cooling, keeps for months*

Used in [Beef Teriyaki Stir-Fry](beef-teriyaki-stir-fry.md).

## Ingredients

- 1 cup sake
- 1 cup mirin
- 1 cup soy sauce
- ½ cup packed brown sugar (white works too)
- 2 garlic cloves, peeled and left whole
- 1 2-inch piece fresh ginger, sliced
- 1 small chunk of carrot
- 2 garlic cloves, finely minced
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, finely minced
- 1 tsp rice vinegar
- 2 tbsp cornstarch
- 2 tbsp cold water

## Steps

1. **Prep the aromatics.** Slice the ginger piece, peel the whole garlic, mince the rest of the garlic and ginger, and cut the carrot chunk. *(timer: 5 min)*
2. **Bring to a boil.** Combine sake, mirin, soy sauce, brown sugar, whole garlic, sliced ginger, carrot, minced garlic, minced ginger and rice vinegar in a medium saucepan. Mark the starting depth on a chopstick, then bring to a boil over medium-high, stirring once or twice to dissolve the sugar. *(timer: 8 min)*
3. **Simmer and reduce.** Drop to a gentle simmer until the aromatics have infused and the sauce is down to about 2½ cups, roughly three-quarters of the starting depth. Start checking at 35 minutes. *(timer: 45 min)*
4. **Pull the whole aromatics.** Fish out and discard the ginger slices, whole garlic cloves and carrot. The minced garlic and ginger stay in. *(timer: 1 min)*
5. **Thicken.** Whisk the cornstarch into the cold water until smooth, then stir it into the simmering sauce. Cook, stirring, until glossy and thick enough to coat the back of a spoon. *(timer: 2 min)*
6. **Cool and store.** Cool to room temperature (a wide bowl speeds it up), then jar and refrigerate. It keeps for several months. *(timer: 45 min)*

## Notes

Timing: about 5 minutes of prep, 8 to boil, 45 unattended at a simmer, 3 to finish, then about 45 to cool before jarring. It fits around another weekend cook.

Kenji's recipe doesn't say what to do with the carrot chunk; it comes out with the other whole aromatics.

A half batch reduces faster; start checking at 25 minutes. Plan on about ⅔ cup per stir-fry for four, so a full batch covers roughly four dinners. Cold from the fridge it's stiff; whisk in a splash of water before it hits a pan.

Buy mirin that lists mirin as the first ingredient rather than corn syrup. Inexpensive junmai sake is fine.

Optional additions to the simmer: ½ cup pineapple juice, 1 tsp toasted sesame oil, scallion or onion scraps, or mushroom trimmings. Kenji also suggests chili garlic paste; leave it out to keep it mild.

Source: J. Kenji López-Alt, [Recipe: Seattle-Style Teriyaki Sauce](https://frienji.kenjilopezalt.com/posts/recipe-seattle-162546101) (Frienji membership, free).
