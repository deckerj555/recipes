# Pasta alla Norma

*Sicilian eggplant and tomato pasta — silky fried eggplant, bright tomato sauce, salty cheese. No chili at all.*

## Ingredients

- 1½ lb Japanese eggplant, cut into 3/4-inch half-moons
- ½ cup olive oil, for frying (approx.)
- 2 lb ripe heirloom or cherry tomatoes
- 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1 lb dry pasta (creste di gallo, rigatoni, or similar ridged shape)
- 1 cup fresh basil leaves, torn
- 3 oz ricotta salata (or pecorino/aged feta), coarsely grated
- 2 tsp kosher salt, divided, plus more for pasta water
- ½ tsp dried oregano (optional)

## Steps

1. **Salt the eggplant.** Toss Japanese eggplant with half of kosher salt in a colander and let sit 20–30 minutes to draw out moisture. Pat very dry — dry eggplant fries instead of steaming and drinks less oil. *(timer: 25 min)*
2. **Prep the tomatoes.** If using heirlooms: score, blanch 30 seconds in boiling water, peel, and roughly chop. If using cherry tomatoes: just halve them. Save the boiling water for the pasta.
3. **Fry the eggplant.** Heat olive oil in a wide skillet over medium-high. Fry the eggplant in batches, without crowding, until deep golden and creamy inside, 3–4 minutes per side. Drain on a rack or paper towels and salt lightly. *(timer: 8 min)*
4. **Make the tomato sauce.** Pour off all but 3 tablespoons of the frying oil. Over medium heat, soften garlic cloves until fragrant and just golden, about 1 minute. Add the tomatoes, the remaining kosher salt, and dried oregano. Simmer, crushing with a spoon, until saucy and slightly thickened, 15–20 minutes. *(timer: 18 min)*
5. **Cook the pasta.** Meanwhile, cook pasta in well-salted boiling water to 2 minutes shy of the package time. Reserve a cup of pasta water before draining. *(timer: 9 min)*
6. **Marry everything.** Add the pasta to the sauce with a splash of pasta water and toss over medium heat until glossy and clinging, 1–2 minutes. Fold in the fried eggplant and most of basil, gently so the eggplant stays intact. *(timer: 2 min)*
7. **Serve.** Plate and shower generously with ricotta salata and the remaining basil, plus a thread of good olive oil.

## Notes

Market shopping: Japanese eggplant (Killer Tomato), heirloom tomatoes (Feather Oak or Killer Tomato's 2# tray), basil (Haylift), and Seven Sisters creste di gallo — its ruffles trap the sauce beautifully. Ricotta salata is the classic but hard to find locally; a dryish feta or pecorino romano is a fine stand-in. Traditional Norma has no chili, so nothing to remove here.
