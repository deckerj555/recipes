# Poached Quince with Italian Sausage & Roasted Brussels Sprouts

*A savory autumn plate: quince poached with bay and thyme, browned Italian sausage, and hard-roasted sprouts, tied together with a pan sauce built from the poaching liquid.*

## Ingredients

- 1 quince (about 1 lb), peeled, quartered, cored, each quarter halved
- 1 firm apple (Jonagold or Braeburn), peeled and cut the same size — to stretch the quince
- 2 cups water
- 3 tbsp sugar
- 1 lemon: a wide strip of peel plus 1 tbsp of the juice
- 1 bay leaf
- 4 thyme sprigs, divided
- 1½ lb Italian herb sausages (Stamboom), about 6 links
- 1½ lb Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 large shallot, thinly sliced
- 1 tbsp butter
- 1 tbsp cider vinegar
- 1½ tsp kosher salt, divided
- ½ tsp black pepper

## Steps

1. **Poach the quince.** Combine the water, sugar, lemon peel and juice, bay leaf, and 2 thyme sprigs in a small saucepan and bring to a simmer. Add the quince — the liquid should nearly cover it. Lower the heat so it barely bubbles, cover partially, and cook until a knife slides in with no resistance and the fruit has turned amber or rosy. Add the apple for the last 12 minutes so it doesn't collapse. Raw quince is hard and astringent and will not soften alongside the sausages the way apple does; this long gentle simmer is what breaks down the tannins and turns the texture silky. *(timer: 55 min)*
2. **Reduce the poaching liquid.** Lift the fruit out with a slotted spoon and set aside. Strain the liquid, return it to the pan, and boil it down to about ½ cup of light syrup. This becomes the pan sauce. *(timer: 7 min)*
3. **Roast the Brussels sprouts.** Heat the oven to 425°F. Toss the sprouts with the olive oil, 1 tsp of the salt, and the pepper. Spread on a sheet pan cut-side down in a single layer. Roast undisturbed for the first 15 minutes, until the cut faces are deeply browned and the outer leaves are crisp. *(timer: 25 min)*
4. **Brown the sausages.** Heat a large skillet over medium. Cook the sausages, turning every few minutes, until browned on all sides and 160°F inside. Lower the heat if they color faster than they cook — split casings dump out the fat and the juices with it. Move to a plate. *(timer: 14 min)*
5. **Warm the quince with shallot.** Pour off all but a tablespoon of fat. Add the shallot and the leaves from the remaining 2 thyme sprigs and cook over medium until soft and golden, about 3 minutes. Add the poached quince and apple and let them take on a little color. *(timer: 5 min)*
6. **Make the pan sauce.** Pour in the reduced syrup and the cider vinegar, scraping up the browned bits. Simmer until slightly thickened, then swirl in the butter off the heat. Taste: it should read tart and savory, not sweet. Adjust with more vinegar or a pinch of salt. *(timer: 3 min)*
7. **Serve.** Return the sausages to the pan and turn them in the sauce for a minute to glaze. Plate with the roasted sprouts, spooning the quince, shallots, and sauce over everything. *(timer: 1 min)*

## Notes

The sugar is deliberately about a third of what a dessert poach uses, and the bay and thyme pull in the same direction — you want the quince fragrant and barely sweet so it sits beside the sausage instead of turning the plate into dessert.

Quince is genuinely hard to cut. Use the heaviest knife in the drawer, halve it through the stem end with steady downward pressure rather than a chop, and peel after halving. The cores are large and woody; cut generously around them.

The co-op usually has only a few quince at a time, hence the apple to make up the volume. The quince is still what you taste — it perfumes everything cooked with it. With two quince, drop the apple.

Make-ahead: the quince can be poached up to three days in advance and kept in its liquid in the fridge, where it improves. That turns this into a 35-minute dinner on the night.

Sourcing: Stamboom Italian herb sausage, quince and apples from The Farmer in Odell, Brussels sprouts from Nature's Finest, shallots from M&P, thyme from Killer Tomato.

## With ginger and sage sausage

Stamboom's ginger and sage sausage is an even better match than the Italian herb — sage and quince are a classic pair, and the ginger echoes the fruit's floral note. No other changes.
