# Beef and Cabbage Stir-Fry

*Velveted beef with cabbage, carrots, and scallions in a savory soy-ginger sauce — 25 minutes, no heat*

## Ingredients

- 1¼ lb sirloin tip steak (or top sirloin), sliced thin across the grain
- 1 tsp baking soda (for the velveting)
- 1 tbsp cornstarch, divided
- ½ small green cabbage, cored and cut into 1-inch squares
- 2 carrots, sliced thin on a sharp diagonal
- 6 scallions, whites cut into 1-inch lengths, greens sliced thin and reserved
- 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, finely grated
- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine or dry sherry
- 1 tsp sugar
- ½ cup water or unsalted stock
- 2 tsp toasted sesame oil
- 3 tbsp neutral oil, divided
- 3 cups short-grain or jasmine rice, cooked

## Steps

1. **Velvet the beef.** Toss the beef with the baking soda and 2 teaspoons of water until every slice is coated. Let it sit 30 minutes at room temperature. Rinse thoroughly under cold water, drain, and pat very dry — leftover soda tastes metallic. Toss with 2 teaspoons of the cornstarch. *(timer: 30 min)*
2. **Cook the rice and prep everything.** Start the rice now so it's ready when the stir-fry is. Meanwhile cut the cabbage, the carrots, the scallions, the garlic, and grate the ginger. Set everything within arm's reach of the stove — once the pan is hot there's no time to go chopping. *(timer: 20 min)*
3. **Mix the sauce.** Whisk soy sauce, oyster sauce, Shaoxing wine or dry sherry, sugar, water, and the remaining 1 teaspoon of the cornstarch in a small bowl. Keep it by the stove and re-whisk before it goes in — cornstarch settles fast.
4. **Sear the beef in two batches.** Heat a wok or large skillet over the highest heat until it smokes. Add 1 tablespoon of the neutral oil, then half the beef in a single layer. Leave it undisturbed 45 seconds to brown, toss 30 seconds more, and remove while still slightly underdone. Repeat with another tablespoon of oil and the rest of the beef. *(timer: 4 min)*
5. **Stir-fry the vegetables.** Add the last tablespoon of the neutral oil. Toss in the carrots and the scallion whites and stir-fry 90 seconds. Add the cabbage and cook, tossing often, until it's wilted at the edges but still has snap, 2–3 minutes. Add the garlic and the ginger for the last 30 seconds. *(timer: 5 min)*
6. **Sauce and finish.** Return the beef and any juices to the pan. Re-whisk the sauce and pour it around the edges of the pan so it hits hot metal. Toss constantly until it thickens and coats everything, about 1 minute. Off heat, stir in sesame oil. *(timer: 1 min 30 sec)*
7. **Serve.** Scatter the reserved scallion greens over the top and serve immediately over the rice.

## Notes

VELVETING is what separates restaurant stir-fry from home stir-fry, and it's the one step worth not skipping. The baking soda raises the meat's surface pH so the proteins don't seize and squeeze out water — you get tender, silky slices from a cheap cut instead of chewy gray ones. Thirty minutes is plenty; past an hour the texture goes soft and slightly soapy, so rinse on time.

SLICE THIN. Freeze the steak 30–45 minutes until firm at the edges and it's easy to get 1/8-inch slices. Always across the grain.

BATCHES, NOT CROWDING. A home burner can't keep up with a full pan, and crowded beef steams gray. Two batches, thirty seconds of patience each, and you get real browning.

Everything here except the pantry items is on the current market list: sirloin tip steak (Kelly & Friends, $11.50), green cabbage and carrots (Nature's Finest), scallions (Gargoyle Gardens). Swap the cabbage for savoy if you'd rather — it's more tender and cooks a minute faster.

Variations: swap in shiitake for a meatier version, or add the sauce to leftover rice the next day for fried rice.

Timing: the velveting and the rice both want to start about 45 minutes before dinner, and the actual cooking is under ten minutes once the pan is hot.
