# Petrale Sole Meunière with Glazed Salad Turnips

*Classic browned-butter-and-lemon sole with glazed salad turnips and garlicky spinach — 25 minutes start to finish*

## Ingredients

- 4 petrale sole fillets (12–16 oz each), thawed and patted very dry
- ½ cup all-purpose flour, for dredging
- 8 tbsp butter, divided
- 2 tbsp neutral oil, divided
- 1 bunch salad turnips, greens trimmed, turnips halved (quartered if large)
- 8 oz spinach
- 2 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 2 lemons: 1 juiced, 1 cut into wedges
- 2 tbsp capers, drained (optional)
- 2 tbsp fresh Italian parsley, chopped (optional)
- 1½ tsp kosher salt, divided
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp sugar (for the turnip glaze)

## Steps

1. **Glaze the turnips.** Put the salad turnips in a wide skillet with water to come halfway up, 2 tablespoons of the butter, sugar, and 1/2 teaspoon of the salt. Simmer briskly, uncovered, until the water cooks away and the turnips are tender and glazed, 10–12 minutes. Shake the pan at the end to coat. Set aside. *(timer: 11 min)*
2. **Wilt the spinach.** In another pan, heat 1 tablespoon of the oil over medium-high with the garlic for 30 seconds, then add spinach in handfuls and toss until just wilted, about 2 minutes. Season lightly, drain off any liquid, and keep warm. *(timer: 2 min 30 sec)*
3. **Season and dredge.** Season the sole fillets on both sides with the remaining the salt and black pepper. Spread the flour on a plate and dredge each fillet right before it goes in the pan, pressing lightly and shaking off all excess — you want a bare dusting, not a coating.
4. **Sauté the sole.** Heat 2 tablespoons of the butter with the remaining the oil in a large skillet over medium-high until the butter foams. Lay in the fillets skinned-side up and cook undisturbed 2–3 minutes until golden at the edges. Flip gently with a fish spatula and cook 1–2 minutes more. Move to warm plates. *(timer: 4 min 30 sec)*
5. **Brown the butter.** Wipe the pan if the flour has darkened. Add the remaining the butter and cook over medium, swirling, until it foams, then turns hazelnut brown and smells toasty, 2–3 minutes. Watch it closely — it turns from brown to burnt in seconds. *(timer: 3 min)*
6. **Finish the sauce.** Off the heat, add the juice from the lemon — it will sputter hard — along with capers and parsley. Swirl to combine.
7. **Serve immediately.** Spoon the browned butter over the fillets while it's still foaming. Plate the glazed turnips and spinach alongside, with lemon wedges.

## Notes

DRY FISH IS EVERYTHING. Petrale sole is thin and wet from thawing; wet fish steams and the flour turns to paste. Thaw in the fridge overnight, then blot hard with paper towels on both sides. Dredge at the last second — flour sitting on damp fish goes gummy.

Petrale cooks in about 4 minutes total. Have the turnips, spinach, and plates ready before the fish hits the pan; this is not a dish you can walk away from. If your skillet only holds two fillets, cook in two batches and hold the first pair on a warm plate — they're fine for five minutes.

Salad turnips (sometimes called Hakurei) are new at the market this week and worth trying: crisp, sweet, and mild, closer to a radish than a storage turnip. Raw in the slaw they're good too. Their greens are edible — sauté them with the spinach if they look fresh.

The classic garnish is parsley and capers; there's one bunch of parsley left at the market. Without it, lemon and browned butter carry the dish on their own.
