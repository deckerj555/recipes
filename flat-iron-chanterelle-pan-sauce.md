# Flat Iron Steak with Chanterelle Pan Sauce

*Seared flat iron with a butter-and-chanterelle pan sauce, crushed potatoes, and wilted spinach*

## Ingredients

- 4 flat iron steaks (about 1/2 lb each)
- 1 lb chanterelle mushrooms, torn into bite-size pieces
- 2 lb Gala Yellow potatoes
- 8 oz spinach
- 5 tbsp butter, divided
- 2 tbsp olive oil, divided
- 1 small shallot or 1/4 sweet onion, finely diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced, divided
- ⅓ cup dry white wine or beef stock
- ¼ cup heavy cream (optional, for a richer sauce)
- 2 tbsp fresh Italian parsley, chopped
- 2 tsp kosher salt, divided, plus more to taste
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 lemon, half juiced

## Steps

1. **Boil the potatoes.** Cover the potatoes with cold water in a pot, add 1 teaspoon of the salt, and boil until a knife slides through easily, 18–22 minutes. Drain well and let them steam dry a minute in the hot pot. *(timer: 20 min)*
2. **Smash the potatoes.** Return the potatoes to the pot with 2 tablespoons of the butter, a third of the garlic, and salt. Crush them roughly with a fork or masher — lumpy is the goal, not smooth. Cover and keep warm.
3. **Season the steaks.** Pat the steaks completely dry and season generously on both sides with the remaining the salt and black pepper. Let them sit at room temperature while the potatoes cook — a cold steak sears unevenly. *(timer: 20 min)*
4. **Sear the steaks.** Heat 1 tablespoon of the olive oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high until just smoking. Sear the steaks 3–4 minutes per side for medium-rare (130°F), working in two batches so the pan stays hot. Transfer to a plate and let rest at least 8 minutes. *(timer: 10 min)*
5. **Dry-sauté, then brown the chanterelles.** Wipe the pan but keep the browned bits. Over medium-high, add the chanterelles to the DRY pan and cook, stirring occasionally, until they squeak and release their water and the pan goes dry again, 4–5 minutes. Now add 2 tablespoons of the butter and cook until deeply golden and the edges crisp, 4–5 minutes more. *(timer: 10 min)*
6. **Build the pan sauce.** Add the shallot and half the remaining the garlic; cook 2 minutes until soft. Pour in the wine or stock, scraping up the fond, and simmer until reduced by half. Stir in the cream if using, plus any juices from the resting steak, and simmer 2 minutes until it coats a spoon. Off heat, add the remaining the butter, a squeeze of lemon, and parsley. *(timer: 6 min)*
7. **Wilt the spinach.** In a separate wide pan, heat the remaining the olive oil with the last of the garlic over medium-high. Add spinach in handfuls, tossing until just wilted, about 2 minutes. Salt lightly and squeeze on a little more lemon. *(timer: 2 min)*
8. **Serve.** Slice the steaks across the grain. Plate the smashed potatoes and spinach, lay the steak alongside, and spoon the chanterelles and all their sauce over the top.

## Notes

CLEANING CHANTERELLES: brush or wipe them, don't soak — they're sponges and waterlogged chanterelles won't brown. A quick rinse is fine if they're gritty, but pat dry immediately and let them sit out 15 minutes before cooking. Tear rather than slice; the ragged edges catch more butter.

DRY SAUTÉ FIRST: chanterelles hold a surprising amount of water. Starting them in a dry pan drives it off, and only then do they brown in butter instead of steaming in their own liquid. This is the one step people skip and then wonder why their expensive mushrooms taste bland.

Flat iron is a chuck cut with tenderness close to a ribeye at a third the price. Pull at 130°F for medium-rare and rest a full 8 minutes — it's a well-marbled cut that keeps cooking off the heat. Slice across the grain, which runs lengthwise down the steak.

Parsley is nearly gone at the market (one bunch at Gargoyle Gardens), so grab it early or leave it out — the sauce is fine without. No thyme this week, which is a shame here; basil is the wrong direction for this one, so skip herbs rather than substitute.
