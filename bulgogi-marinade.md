# Bulgogi Marinade

*Classic sweet-savory Korean marinade — enough for about 2 lb of thinly sliced beef*

Used in [Bulgogi Night with Banchan](bulgogi-night-with-banchan.md).

## Ingredients

- ½ Asian pear (or 1 small Bartlett pear), peeled and grated
- ⅓ cup soy sauce
- 3 tbsp brown sugar (or 2 Tbsp sugar + 1 Tbsp honey)
- 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil
- 2 tbsp mirin or rice wine
- 6 garlic cloves, finely grated
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, finely grated
- ¼ yellow onion, grated
- 3 spring onions, thinly sliced
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
- ½ tsp black pepper

## Steps

1. **Grate the pear and onion.** Grate the pear and the onion on a box grater into a bowl, catching all the juice. The pear does double duty here — sweetness plus enzymes that tenderize the meat.
2. **Whisk in the rest.** Add soy sauce, brown sugar, toasted sesame oil, mirin or rice wine, garlic cloves, fresh ginger, and black pepper. Whisk until the sugar is fully dissolved.
3. **Finish.** Stir in spring onions and toasted sesame seeds. Taste — it should be salty-sweet and unmistakably garlicky. Adjust sugar or soy to your liking.
4. **Use or store.** Use right away as a marinade, or refrigerate up to 5 days. It also keeps well frozen; freeze it in a bag with the meat already in it and it marinates as it thaws.

## Notes

The grated pear is the real signature here — its enzymes tenderize the beef and it brings a fruity sweetness sugar alone can't. Killer Tomato and Allegre Farms both have Hosui Asian pears right now; Bartlett works in a pinch. For meat: thin-sliced ribeye or sirloin is classic (freeze 30–45 minutes to slice paper-thin), but skirt steak from Little Seven Seven or Bowdish is excellent — slice it across the grain after cooking rather than before. Marinate 2–4 hours for thin slices, up to overnight for thicker cuts; the pear enzymes will turn the texture mushy past about 12 hours. Cook hot and fast on a grill or ripping-hot cast iron, in batches so it sears instead of steams. The same sauce works on pork shoulder (dweji bulgogi) and chicken thighs. No chili in this version at all — gochujang is a common addition but isn't traditional in beef bulgogi.
