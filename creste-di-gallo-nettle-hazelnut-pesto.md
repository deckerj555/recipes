# Creste di Gallo with Nettle-Hazelnut Pesto, Brussels Sprouts & Carrots

*Ruffled pasta tossed with nettle-hazelnut pesto, charred Brussels sprouts, and sweet roasted carrots*

## Ingredients

- 1 lb dry pasta, creste di gallo (or radiatori or lumache)
- 1 lb Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved
- ¾ lb carrots, cut into 1/2-inch oblique chunks
- 3 tbsp olive oil, divided
- 1 tsp kosher salt, divided, plus more for the pasta water
- ¾ jar nettle-hazelnut pesto (about 7 oz)
- 2 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 2 tbsp butter
- 2 tsp lemon, zested and juiced
- 2 tbsp roasted hazelnuts, roughly chopped
- 2 oz Parmesan or pecorino, finely grated, plus more to serve
- ¼ tsp black pepper

## Steps

1. **Heat the oven and prep the vegetables.** Heat the oven to 425°F. Toss Brussels sprouts and carrots with 2 tablespoons of olive oil and half of kosher salt on a sheet pan. Spread them out with the sprouts cut-side down — crowding steams them instead of roasting.
2. **Roast until charred.** Roast 22–28 minutes, undisturbed for the first 15, until the sprout cut-faces are deeply browned and the carrots are tender with caramelized edges. Don't stir early; the char is the point. *(timer: 25 min)*
3. **Cook the pasta.** Meanwhile, bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil and cook the pasta to 1 minute shy of the package time. Reserve 1.5 cups of pasta water, then drain. *(timer: 10 min)*
4. **Bloom the garlic.** In a wide skillet over medium-low, warm the remaining olive oil with garlic cloves and cook gently until just golden, about 90 seconds. Add butter and swirl until melted. *(timer: 2 min)*
5. **Toss with pesto.** Off the heat, add the drained pasta to the skillet along with the pesto and a big splash of pasta water. Toss hard until the pesto loosens into a sauce that coats every ruffle, adding more water as needed. Keeping it off direct heat preserves the pesto's color and flavor.
6. **Fold in the vegetables.** Fold in the roasted vegetables, lemon zest and juice, Parmesan, black pepper, and the remaining kosher salt. Taste and adjust — it usually wants more lemon and more salt than you'd expect.
7. **Serve.** Plate and finish with roasted hazelnuts and another shower of cheese.

## Notes

Heat the oven fully before the sprouts go in — 425°F is what gets the leaves crisp instead of steamy. Cut sides down and undisturbed for the first 15 minutes is the whole trick. Never heat the pesto directly in the pan; the residual heat of the pasta is enough, and cooking it dulls the herbs and turns them army-green. Market shopping: Seven Sisters creste di gallo and nettle-hazelnut pesto, Nature's Finest Brussels sprouts (first of the season), carrots from any of four growers, and Farmhands roasted hazelnuts are all on the list. To make it a fuller meal, torn Stamboom prosciutto crisped in the pan is excellent, or leave it vegetarian as written.
