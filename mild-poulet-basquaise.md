# Mild Poulet Basquaise

*Basque chicken braised in sweet-pepper piperade — sweet paprika stands in for hot Espelette pepper*

## Ingredients

- 3 lb chicken legs or bone-in thighs
- 4 sweet red peppers (Carmen or Jimmy Nardello), sliced into strips
- 1 green bell pepper, sliced into strips
- 2 sweet onions, thinly sliced
- 4 garlic cloves, sliced
- 1½ lb ripe tomatoes, peeled and chopped
- 2 tsp sweet paprika (or Habanada chili powder)
- ½ cup dry white wine
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 bay leaf
- 3 thyme sprigs
- 2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
- 2 oz prosciutto or thin-cut ham, torn (optional but traditional)

## Steps

1. **Brown the chicken.** Season chicken all over with kosher salt. Heat olive oil in a wide Dutch oven over medium-high and brown the chicken skin-side down until deep golden, 6–8 minutes; flip and brown 3 more. Set aside on a plate. *(timer: 10 min)*
2. **Start the piperade.** In the same pot over medium heat, cook sweet onions and prosciutto until the onions are soft and translucent, about 8 minutes. Add sweet red peppers, green bell pepper, and garlic cloves and cook until the peppers begin to slump, another 8–10 minutes. *(timer: 18 min)*
3. **Bloom the paprika and deglaze.** Stir in sweet paprika and cook 30 seconds — don't let it scorch. Pour in dry white wine and scrape up the browned bits; simmer until mostly evaporated, about 2 minutes. *(timer: 2 min 30 sec)*
4. **Braise.** Add ripe tomatoes, bay leaf, and thyme sprigs. Nestle the chicken skin-side up into the peppers along with any juices. Partially cover and simmer gently until the chicken is tender and the sauce has thickened, 35–40 minutes. *(timer: 37 min 30 sec)*
5. **Finish and serve.** Discard the bay leaf and thyme stems. Taste the piperade and adjust salt — it should be sweet, silky, and rich. Serve the chicken over rice or with crusty bread, peppers spooned generously on top. *(timer: 2 min)*

## Notes

Market shopping: Carmen and Jimmy Nardello peppers (Killer Tomato) are ideal here — both are genuinely sweet frying peppers with zero heat. Green bell from Gathering Together, sweet onions from Nature's Finest, tomatoes from Feather Oak, thyme from Haylift, and Stamboom's prosciutto for the traditional ham note. Classic Basquaise uses piment d'Espelette, which is gently warm; sweet paprika or Hayshaker's Habanada powder gives you the pepper flavor with none of it. The piperade alone is worth mastering — leftover, it's the base for eggs the next morning.
